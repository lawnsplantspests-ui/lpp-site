First: Keep the Dog Away From That Side of the House.

Almost every skunk emergency we get called into started with a dog. Skunks spray accurately out to roughly ten to fifteen feet, and they give plenty of warning first — stamping the front feet, raising the tail — which a dog reads as an invitation. Until the animal is out, gate that section off.

Second thing: don't block the hole yet. It's the natural instinct and it's the one move that reliably makes this worse. More on that below.

How to Tell It's a Skunk

You often never see the animal. What you have is a hole and a smell, and several things dig holes around here.

  • The hole. Roughly eight inches across, dug in at the edge of a structure — deck skirting, porch step, shed corner, slab edge — with a fan of loose soil pushed out behind it.
  • The smell. A low, musky background odor near that corner even when nothing has actually sprayed. Skunks smell faintly of skunk all the time.
  • The timing. Movement at dusk and again just before dawn. Skunks are not daytime animals; a skunk out wandering at midday is worth calling about for other reasons.
  • The lawn. Small cone-shaped divots appearing overnight across the turf. That's a skunk nosing out grubs, and it's often the first sign people notice.

Skunk

Hole at the edge of a structure, musky smell, dusk and dawn activity, cone-shaped divots in the lawn. Dens under decks, porches, sheds, and slabs.

Groundhog

Bigger hole, usually 10–12 inches, with a substantial dirt mound, and active in daylight. Often under a shed or deck as well, which is why the two get confused. See groundhog under your shed or deck.

Opossum

Frequently uses a hole something else dug rather than digging its own. No musky den smell. Generally the least destructive of the three and sometimes worth simply letting move on.

Raccoon

Less likely to be under a deck than up in a soffit, attic, or chimney — but it happens. Torn or pried entry rather than dug. See raccoon in your attic or chimney.

Spring timing matters more than anything else on this page Skunks breed in late winter and kits are typically in the den from around April into June. During that window the mother can come and go but the young cannot. Any exclusion done then seals kits in. If you're calling in spring, say so — it changes the whole approach, and sometimes the right answer is to wait a few weeks.

Please Don't Try These

  • Sealing the hole. If the animal is inside, it digs out somewhere else — or it doesn't, and now you have something dead under your deck in July. If there are kits, it's worse.
  • Mothballs, ammonia rags, or peppermint oil. These get recommended constantly online. At best they push the animal a few feet further under the deck where you can't reach it. Mothballs used this way are also an off-label pesticide use.
  • Flooding the burrow. Doesn't work, undermines whatever the den is dug against, and can move footings.
  • Live-trapping it yourself. Setting a trap is the easy part. Approaching an occupied skunk trap is not, and Pennsylvania has specific rules about what may then be done with the animal. This is the main reason people call us at 11pm.
  • Letting the dog handle it. Self-explanatory, and yet.

How We Handle It

The work is in two halves, and skipping the second is why skunks come back.

Removal. We set traps positioned and covered for the situation, check them on the schedule the regulations require, and handle the animal. Skunks are a rabies vector species in Pennsylvania, which limits what a licensed operator is permitted to do with a trapped one — it is genuinely not the same as relocating a squirrel. We'll walk you through exactly what state rules allow for your situation before a single trap goes out. No surprises on that.

Exclusion. Then we close it properly. For a deck or shed that normally means trenching a barrier down and out along the perimeter so nothing can simply re-dig the same entrance — skunks, groundhogs, and everything else in the neighborhood will happily reuse an existing den. Without this step you are treating a location, not solving a problem, and something will be back under there within a season or two.

Estimates are free. Cost depends on the structure, the number of entry points, and how much of the perimeter needs closing — a shed corner is a very different job from a full wraparound deck. Getting the Smell Out

If something has actually sprayed near the house or got your dog, tomato juice does essentially nothing. The mixture that works is hydrogen peroxide, baking soda, and a little dish soap, mixed fresh and used immediately — it chemically alters the thiols rather than masking them. Don't store it in a sealed container. For a dog, keep it well away from the eyes, and call your vet if there was a direct facial hit.

Frequently Asked Questions

I saw a skunk out in the middle of the day. Is that bad?
It's worth paying attention to. Skunks are normally nocturnal, and daytime activity is one of the signs associated with rabies — though a mother foraging hard in spring is also a perfectly ordinary explanation. If the animal is disoriented, staggering, unusually bold, or aggressive, keep people and pets well away and contact the Pennsylvania Game Commission. Don't approach it to check.
Can you get it out this week?
Usually we can get traps out quickly. But trapping is not instant — it depends on the animal, and legally the traps have to be checked on a set schedule, so this is a job measured in days rather than hours. If you're in the spring window with possible young in the den, we may recommend waiting a few weeks and doing the exclusion properly rather than rushing it.
Why can't you just relocate it like a squirrel?
Because Pennsylvania treats rabies vector species differently, and skunks are one. A licensed NWCO doesn't have the same options with a skunk as with a gray squirrel. We'd rather tell you that plainly up front than take the job and be vague about it.
Something is digging small cone-shaped holes all over my lawn.
That's a skunk or a raccoon hunting grubs, and the grubs are the actual problem — the digging stops when the food does. See something tore my lawn up overnight, which covers both halves of that.
