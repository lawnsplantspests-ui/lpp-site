What We Treat

Yellow Jackets

The most aggressive and most common stinging-insect call we get from late summer through fall. They build nests in the ground, wall voids, soffits, and under attic insulation. By August a single colony can have 2,000–5,000 workers. We treat at the entry point. A nest inside a structure takes 7 to 10 days for full control — workers keep coming and going and carrying material back in, and that is what reaches the whole colony. Expect activity during that window; it does not mean the treatment failed. We treat most if not all yellow jacket nests we're called to — this is not an insect we recommend living alongside, and an active colony needs to be exterminated and the nest removed. What we don't do is sealing and repair work: closing the void, replacing siding, or patching the structure afterward is a job for you or a contractor, and we'll show you exactly where and what's needed rather than quote it ourselves.

Key FactsActive nests peak August through October in Central PA. Yellow jackets sting repeatedly — unlike honey bees which sting once. Allergic reactions are most common with yellow jackets. DIY spray often pushes them deeper into wall voids and creates a worse problem.

Bald-Faced Hornets

Build the iconic football-shaped gray paper nest hanging from tree limbs, under eaves, in gutters, and on houses. Despite the name they're actually a type of yellow jacket — but larger, more aggressive, and their sting carries more venom. A typical mature nest holds 400–700 workers and can be the size of a basketball.

Paper Wasps

Build open-cell umbrella-shaped nests under deck rails, eaves, soffits, mailboxes, grills, swing sets, and porch lights. Smaller colonies than yellow jackets — usually 20–30 workers. Less aggressive overall, but they will defend the nest if disturbed.

European Hornets

Large (up to 1.5 inches), reddish-brown and yellow, active at night — often drawn to lit windows. Typically nest in hollow trees, attic spaces, and wall voids. Less aggressive than yellow jackets but their large size makes them alarming.

Cicada Killers

Large (1.5–2 inches), striking yellow-and-black solitary wasps that dig burrows in lawn areas. Despite their intimidating size they're not aggressive — males can't sting at all. Cicada killers specifically are the one stinging insect where doing nothing is often the right answer — they're solitary, the males can't sting at all, and the burrows are gone by September. We treat them when activity is heavy in a high-traffic area and tell you honestly when it isn't worth it. This does not apply to yellow jackets — a yellow jacket nest is a colony that gets more aggressive as the season goes on, and nearly all of them need to be treated and removed. Carpenter Bees

Look like bumble bees but with a shiny hairless abdomen. They drill perfectly round half-inch holes into deck rails, fascia boards, eaves, fence posts, and exposed beams. The problem isn't safety — it's structural damage. We apply insecticidal dust into each active hole and leave the holes open — that's deliberate. Sealing a bee in doesn't kill it; it just makes it chew a new way out, and in fascia or soffit wood that new way can go inward, into the structure. Left open, returning females keep walking through the dust and carrying it down the gallery, which is what reaches the brood. Once the gallery is finished, the holes do need filling — wood putty or caulk, then paint. Unlike a cicada killer burrow in soil, a hole bored into wood doesn't close on its own, and an open gallery is an invitation to next year's females.

Why DIY Often Makes the Problem Worse

  • Store-bought spray on a wall void nest: Yellow jackets get angry, abandon that entry, and chew new exits into your living space. We've responded to homes where 20–50 yellow jackets came out a ceiling vent the next day.
  • Knocking down a nest without treating first: A bald-faced hornet nest knocked down with a broom releases hundreds of angry hornets. They'll defend the location for hours.
  • Hot water or fire on ground nests: Boiling water reaches a few inches before cooling off. Gasoline and fire are dangerous, illegal, and ineffective.
